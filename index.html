A short history of Hangul

Before the 1440s, Korea had no writing system of its own. Educated Koreans wrote using Chinese characters — a system that took years to master and was, in practice, only available to the elite.

In 1443, King Sejong the Great set out to change that. He commissioned a new alphabet designed specifically to be learned quickly by ordinary people, regardless of their level of education. It was completed that year and published in 1446 in a document called Hunminjeongeum, meaning "The Correct Sounds for the Instruction of the People."

Unlike most alphabets, Hangul is featural: the shape of each consonant is based on the position of the tongue, lips, or throat when making that sound. Vowels are built from simple strokes representing the earth, standing man, and the heavens.

Why this matters for youBecause Hangul was designed to be learned quickly, most beginners can sound out any word within their first one or two classes — long before they understand what it means. That's the goal for today.
