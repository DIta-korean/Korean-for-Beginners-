A short history of Hangul

Before the 1440s, Korea had no writing system of its own. In 1443, King Sejong the Great commissioned a new alphabet designed to be learned quickly by ordinary people. It was published in 1446 in the Hunminjeongeum — "The Correct Sounds for the Instruction of the People."

Hangul is featural: each consonant's shape reflects the position of the tongue, lips, or throat when making that sound.

Why this matters for youMost beginners can sound out any word within their first class — long before they understand what it means. That's the goal for today.

Introducing yourself

저는 [이름]이에요 / 예요"I am [name]." Use 이에요 after a name ending in a consonant, 예요 after a name ending in a vowel. Example: 저는 다나예요 (I'm Dana).
이다 / 아니다 — to be / not to be저는 학생이에요 (I'm a student) · 저는 선생님이 아니에요 (I'm not a teacher)
있다 / 없다 — have / don't have저는 시간이 있어요 (I have time) · 저는 시간이 없어요 (I don't have time)
